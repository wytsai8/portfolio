Vision

Towards Personalized Medicine

I envision a future where healthcare becomes proactive, continuous, and personalized. Instead of relying on occasional hospital visits and population-based treatment strategies, individuals will be able to monitor their health status from home using non-invasive or minimally invasive technologies.

In this vision, a home-based health monitoring platform continuously collects multi-modal biological data, including physiological signals, molecular biomarkers, and personal genomic information. Data from biomedical signals such as ECG, PPG, activity monitoring, and emerging sensing technologies, together with proteomic and genomic measurements, are securely integrated and analyzed using advanced machine learning and computational methods.

The goal is not simply to generate more data, but to transform complex, high-dimensional biological information into actionable insights. By integrating multi-omics data, biomedical signals, clinical knowledge, and AI-driven analytics, it becomes possible to identify early signs of disease, discover personalized biomarkers, predict health risks, and provide individualized recommendations before symptoms appear.

While some aspects of this vision already exist today, including wearable devices, continuous physiological monitoring, and consumer genomic testing, the integration of real-time molecular profiling remains one of the major challenges. In particular, routine home-based proteomic monitoring will require breakthroughs in biosensors, microfluidics, and lab-on-a-chip technologies. I believe future healthcare systems will progressively combine genomic profiles, biomarker monitoring, and physiological signals into a unified framework for precision health management.

Ultimately, I believe this is a pathway toward building digital representations of individual health, where data-driven models continuously learn from a person's biological and physiological state. Such systems could support early disease detection, personalized prevention, precision medicine, and better long-term health outcomes.

My research interests in machine learning, bioinformatics, multi-omics integration, and biomedical data analysis are motivated by this long-term vision: translating complex biological data into meaningful insights that empower personalized healthcare for everyone.

Alzheimer’s Disease Biomarker Discovery

Responsibility Built machine learning pipelines to integrate multi-omics and clinical data for Alzheimer’s disease biomarker identification.
Outcome: Developed a machine learning-based computational pipeline integrating 28 large-scale proteomics datasets to distinguish Alzheimer’s disease patients from healthy controls. The analysis incorporated multi-platform proteomics data from brain tissue and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) across heterogeneous cohorts, enabling robust cross-cohort integration and biomarker evaluation. The pipeline further enabled identification of novel biomarker candidates across different proteomics platforms, significantly reducing the time required for experimental biomarker discovery. Using Random Forest and Logistic Regression models, the approach achieved strong predictive performance, with Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (AUC-PR) reaching or exceeding 0.9 across multiple datasets.
1. Tsai WY, Giesbertz, P, Breimann S, Lichtenthaler S, Frishman D. Multi-Platform Integration of Brain and CSF Proteomes Reveals Biomarker Panels for Alzheimer’s Disease. Briefings in Bioinformatics. 2026;27(1)

Biomedical Data and Signal Analysis

Responsibility Designed computational pipelines for biomedical signal analysis, feature extraction, and predictive modeling to identify clinically relevant blood biochemical biomarkers from non-invasive imaging data.

Outcome:
Photoacoustic Imaging: Identified signal-derived biomarkers significantly associated with 14 blood biochemical parameters, including creatinine, uric acid, albumin, and lipid-related markers. Developed statistical prediction models that achieved adjusted R² values up to 0.97, demonstrating the potential of photoacoustic signals for non-invasive biochemical assessment.
Absorption Spectroscopy Imaging: Discovered imaging features significantly associated with 17 blood biochemical parameters and developed feature-based predictive models for key clinical biomarkers. Achieved adjusted R² values above 0.75 for albumin and uric acid, supporting the feasibility of absorption spectroscopy for non-invasive biomarker detection and health monitoring.

2. Tsai WY, Breimann S, Shen TW, Frishman D. Photoacoustic and absorption spectroscopy imaging analysis of human blood. PLoS One. 2023;18(8):e0289704.
