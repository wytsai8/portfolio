Novel Biomarker Discovery for Alzheimer's Disease

Objective: Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a highly heterogeneous neurodegenerative disorder, and currently established biomarkers such as Aβ, Tau, and pTau, while clinically valuable, do not fully capture the complexity of its underlying pathology. To address this challenge, we integrated large-scale proteomics datasets from brain tissue and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) across multiple studies and analytical platforms. By combining proteomic and clinical data with machine learning approaches, we developed a cross-platform biomarker discovery framework to identify protein panels that more accurately distinguish AD patients from healthy controls. This framework is intended to support laboratory research by accelerating the discovery and prioritization of novel AD biomarkers for downstream experimental validation.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed a machine learning framework for integrating multi-platform, multi-cohort proteomics datasets and analyzed 28 large-scale proteomics cohorts comprising approximately 3,000 samples from brain tissue and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). Using this framework, we successfully identified protein biomarker panels capable of distinguishing Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients from healthy controls. The approach achieved strong predictive performance, with the Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (AUC-PR) reaching or exceeding 0.9 across multiple independent datasets. Our results not only validated several well-established AD biomarkers, including APP, GFAP, VGF, HSPB1, and ICAM1, but also identified several novel candidate biomarkers, such as LDHB, PGK1, SOD1, and PPIA, which have not previously been reported as AD-associated proteins. Furthermore, pathway enrichment analysis revealed that these proteins are involved in biological pathways closely related to AD pathogenesis, highlighting their potential as novel biomarkers that warrant further experimental validation.

We also observed that protein expression profiles in brain tissue are strongly influenced by regional and cellular heterogeneity, resulting in lower reproducibility across cohorts and proteomics platforms. In contrast, CSF more comprehensively reflects the overall physiological state of the central nervous system and exhibited greater consistency and overlap of biomarker panels across different analytical platforms and machine learning models. These findings suggest that CSF may represent a more robust source for Alzheimer's disease biomarker discovery and validation. Overall, our study demonstrates the value of combining large-scale proteomics integration with machine learning approaches to advance AD biomarker discovery, early diagnosis, and precision medicine applications.
